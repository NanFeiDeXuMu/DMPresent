    Association rules

    Where a trip starts predicts where it ends

    Every trip becomes a basket of 8 items: day type, time slot, pickup and drop-off borough, distance, total amount, payment and passengers. We mined itemsets with support ≥ 1% and at most 3 items, and ran Apriori and FP-Growth side by side; they returned identical itemsets and counts.     Data quality

    Only of raw records are excluded by default

    Quality checks are kept as flags rather than deleted, so every exclusion can be traced back. Checks can overlap, so the counts do not add up.

    How we built it

    A count-preserving path from raw records to inspectable rules

    1. Clean Validate timestamps, amounts and zone IDs; keep quality flags and source-file hashes.
    2. Encode 8 categorical items per trip; merge identical baskets but keep their counts ( weighted baskets).
    3. Mine twice Apriori and FP-Growth must return identical frequent itemsets () and support counts.
    4. Trace Recount every highlighted rule directly from the cleaned trips and inspect matching records.

    support = n(A and B) / N

    confidence = n(A and B) / n(A)

    lift = confidence / P(B)

    Kulc = (n(AB)/n(A) + n(AB)/n(B)) / 2

    IR = |n(A) − n(B)| / (n(A) + n(B) − n(AB))

    Limits

    One month of data and borough-level rules; the 1% support floor and the three-item limit hide rarer and longer patterns. Recorded trips are not total demand. Rules show co-occurrence, not cause, and cross-month stability has not been tested yet.

    Next steps

    • Freeze rules, bins and thresholds; re-test them on later months and report retention and metric drift.
    • Mine taxi-zone and time-stratified rules, with minimum counts so small zones are not over-read.
    • Host the full analysis app (Streamlit) with versioned data snapshots.
